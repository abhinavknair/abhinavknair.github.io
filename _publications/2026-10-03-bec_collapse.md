The Physics of Bose–Einstein Condensation (BEC) drastically changes when the interaction is switched from repulsive to attractive, giving rise to new phenomena such as the condensate collapse or the so-called “Bosenova”. Numerous theoretical and experimental studies have investigated the collapse of BEC in the past, and an instability condition for BEC was proposed. However, there is a discrepancy between the theoretically predicted and experimentally obtained values of the instability parameter ($k$). In this study, we numerically solve the Gross–Pitaevskii equation (GPE) and find a $k$ value that agrees better than the earlier theoretically predicted one. We incorporate higher-order interaction terms into the GPE and, by accounting for the experimentally observed 25%–40% particle loss on collapse, we find values of $k$ in the range of 0.332–0.415, which closely aligns with the experimentally obtained range. Hence, it is shown that the experimentally obtained range of values of $k$ is better described theoretically when the specific trap geometry, the observed particle loss, and higher-order interaction terms are considered.
